When Words Stop Working. Point instead.

Communication boards and de-escalation, TK through grade 12. Printable boards, staff scripts, and what to do when a student already has a speech device.

During escalation you have two problems, not one. The student cannot get words out, and the student cannot take words in. Every strategy that depends on talking is unavailable at exactly the moment we reach for it hardest.

A communication board is the one channel that stays open. It works because it carries meaning without requiring either person to produce or process speech.

In the moment: three things that work

1
Stop talking.

Three words or fewer. Explaining, reasoning, and restating the rule all add load a student can no longer process. Silence plus a pointed symbol outperforms a correct sentence.

2
Model, don’t demand.

Point to the words while you speak. Never say “use your board” or “use your words.” That converts a support into a compliance demand and adds one more thing the student is failing at.

3
Say yes.

Honor the request every time while you are teaching it. A board honored four times out of five teaches the student that the behavior is the more reliable option.

Where a board fits in the cycle

Boards do different jobs at different points, and they do not work everywhere. Say this part out loud to staff, because anyone who expects a board to stop a peak will decide the tool failed and stop using it.

PhaseWhat the board is for
CalmTeaching. This is the only phase where new learning happens.
TriggerNothing yet. Watch for the body signals the student has named.
AgitationThe student requests an exit, a break, or space.
AccelerationThe adult reduces to two or three symbols. Stop adding words.
PeakNothing works. Safety and presence only. Do not prompt.
De-escalationLow demand. Offer, do not require.
RecoveryThe student explains what happened without producing a full spoken account.

Printable boards

Three tiers, same three functions. Every board is organized by row, and the rows are the whole teaching point: row one refuses, row two asks, row three explains. A staff member who knows that finds a word in about two seconds without reading every cell, and a student who knows it navigates by position instead of scanning.

Notice the ratio. Most cells refuse or request. Only the bottom row names a feeling. A student in acceleration needs a legal way to say “back off,” not a way to label an emotion.

TK through grade 2

Nine cells · half sheet or full page · symbol above text

Refuse stop, no, help
Ask break, my turn, all done
Explain mad, too loud, hurt

Help sits in the refusal row on purpose. In TK it functions as a protest more often than a request. Keep bathroom and drink off this board, since they belong on the classroom schedule and adding them turns a regulation tool into a general request board.

Grades 3 through 5

Twelve cells · desk strip or card ring · symbol above text

Refuse stop, no, later, leave me alone
Ask break, I need space, help, take a walk
Explain mad, frustrated, too loud, difficult

Leave me alone is the cell teams hesitate over and the one students use most. Include it. A student who can say it has a legal alternative to the behavior that otherwise communicates the same thing.

Grades 6 through 12

Nine cells · phrase level · binder card, lanyard, or wallet size

Refuse I need a minute, Not right now, Please back off
Ask I need to step out, I need help, Go get ______
Explain I can’t answer yet, Too much noise in here, Talk about it later

Older students who lose speech under stress rarely need single words. They need a whole utterance they can point to or read aloud without composing it. Leave the adult name blank so each site writes in its own person.

Two cells that prevent referrals. Silence during questioning reads as defiance to almost every adult in a school building. A student who can hand you I can’t answer yet converts a willful defiance write-up into a regulation event. Talk about it later does similar work by making willingness to debrief visible without requiring it now.

If the student has a speech device

Keep it in their hands.

Removing or withholding a device during behavior removes the student’s voice. We would not ask a speaking student to leave theirs in a backpack. If a device is genuinely unsafe in a given moment, the answer is a printed copy of that student’s own pages, not silence.

Do not build a separate de-escalation board for a student who has a robust system. A new layout teaches a competing motor pattern for words the student already has a stored plan for, and under stress stored automaticity carries a person while new learning fails. The board you introduced to help during escalation becomes the one that is unusable during escalation.

Almost everything you need is already on the home page

This is Proloquo2Go, Crescendo Intermediate Core, 60 buttons. The vocabulary is not the problem. The problem is that no adult on the team knows where it lives.

The folder that looks right and is not

Teams navigate to Feelings during behavior because the name sounds like the destination. It is the one page in the system that cannot produce a functional de-escalation message.

What is on this page: angry, frustrated, hurt, sad, afraid, lonely, mean, sick, tired, bored, silly, sorry, excited, happy, plus core want, need, feel, like, hate, not, more, and all done.

What is not: stop, break, wait, help, space, leave me alone, my turn, too loud, later, walk. Every word in the refuse and ask rows of the printed boards is absent, and help is absent entirely.

A student on this page can label an internal state and can say “I don’t like it.” They cannot refuse, request an exit, or ask for a person. Go to Home first. Feelings is a second stop, for the explain function only, after the message is already out.

What to build instead

Eight words on the printed boards are not on the home page: break, wait, space, leave me alone, later, too loud, my turn, and difficult. That gap is why a page is worth building.

Use a Crescendo Activity Template rather than a free-standing folder. Because the template is generated from the home page, core stays in its permanent positions and only the activity slots change. Fill those with the eight words above. Stop, help, go, and People remain exactly where they are on every other page in the system.

Before you add anything, check whether the word already exists somewhere and pull it in from its existing button rather than creating a duplicate in a new position. Wait is most likely in Actions, later in Little Words, loud and quiet in Describe. Open the Help folder at row six, column eight first, because if break already sits there you have a one-navigation path and a shorter template.

Name it something a substitute will find under pressure. Regulation reads clinical. Break and Space is more likely to get tapped.

For a paper backup, do not design one. Print the student’s own pages, or download the Crescendo Quick Communication Boards from AssistiveWare, which already mirror the app and are available in Spanish.

Teaching it, and what to say

A board introduced during a crisis is a piece of paper. Practice on a calm day, in the room where it will be used, with the adults who will be there. Then agree, out loud and as a team, who honors the request and what happens next, because reliability is what makes a board work.

Instead ofDo this
“Use your words.”Point to the word and say it yourself.
“You need to calm down first.”“Break is okay.”
“If you don’t stop, then…”Say nothing. Point to stop.
“Tell me what’s wrong.”“Later. I’ll wait.”
Asking questions at peakStay, reduce input, wait for recovery.

Put it in writing

For a student with an IEP, de-escalation supports belong in the behavior intervention plan and in supplementary aids and services. Undocumented supports are trained inconsistently, disappear when staff change, and leave the student without the protections that come with a written plan. If a board is the plan, write the board into the plan.
